Earn PCN with your graphics card

The PCoin Earner runs AI work on your GPU when you are not using it, and pays you in PCN. It is not mining. Mining competes for a block that one machine wins; this is paid work that somebody actually requested, so every completed job earns — there is no lottery and nothing is wasted on a race you lost.

GPU NVIDIA, 12 GB+ Disk 60 GB free RAM 16 GB+ OS Windows 10/11 Payout in PCN, to your address
Read the pay rate before you install, not after. A card at the 12 GB floor, kept busy for a full day, earns about $2.00 worth of PCN. That is the anchor the whole system is priced from, and it is deliberately public. Your real figure will be lower than that number unless there is work queued every minute of the day, and right now there often is not — an idle earner earns nothing. Treat this as useful work your machine does in gaps, not as an income.

Install it

One file. No installer, no toolchain, nothing from an app store. Put it in a folder you choose and run it — it keeps everything it downloads inside that one folder, so removing it later is deleting the folder.

1

Download the app

PCNEarner.exe  37 KB

It is small because it is only the control panel — the models it runs are downloaded on first start, which is what the 60 GB is for.

2

Windows will warn you

The app is not code-signed, so SmartScreen shows a blue box on anything it has not seen downloaded widely. Click More info, then Run anyway. This is a reputation warning about the certificate, not a detection of anything in the file — the same warning appears on the PCoin node itself. Every file's SHA-256 is published at pc.am/dl/SHA256SUMS.txt if you want to check what you downloaded.

3

Create an account

Pick a username of five characters or more and a password. That account is what your earnings accumulate against, and it lets you run more than one machine under the same balance. The app stores its machine token encrypted with Windows DPAPI, tied to your user profile.

4

Set where you get paid

Paste a PCN address into Payout address. It is checked against the node before it is accepted, so a typo is caught immediately rather than after your first payout goes somewhere unrecoverable. No address, no payout — the work still runs, but nothing can be sent.

5

Press Start

The tray icon turns green while a job is running and amber while paused, so the state is readable at a glance without opening the window. First start takes a while: it downloads the models before it can accept its first job.

Does my machine qualify?

The app checks all of this on first run and tells you which line fails, rather than installing and then quietly never being given work.

Graphics card

NVIDIA with 12 GB of VRAM or more. The reference card is an RTX 3080 Ti, and it is deliberately the floor rather than a high-end target, so the machine everything is priced against is representative. AMD and Intel GPUs are not supported yet. Integrated graphics will not work.

Disk

60 GB free, and it genuinely gets used — the language models alone are tens of gigabytes. You choose the folder, everything lives under it, and the app asks before it grows. More space means more model types you can be given, which means more hours you are eligible for work.

Memory and OS

16 GB of RAM and Windows 10 or 11. It must run from your own desktop session; it refuses to start as a service, because a tray app launched by a service is invisible to the person at the keyboard and there would be no way to tell it apart from broken.

Electricity

A 12 GB card under load draws roughly 250–350 W. Check what a kilowatt-hour costs where you live before you leave it running overnight. In some places that is comfortably under the $2.00/day ceiling; in others it is not. We would rather you did that sum first.

How you are paid

One anchor, one formula, and no per-task haggling: 

reference_seconds  =  how long the job takes on the reference card
cost_usd           =  reference_seconds / 3600 x $0.08333
paid_in            =  PCN, converted at the rate when the job was quoted

Seconds are measured on the reference card, not on yours. That sounds unfair and is the opposite: if you were paid for the seconds your card spent, anyone could underclock theirs and charge three times as much for the same job, and nothing observable would tell that apart from an honest slow machine. Measuring against a fixed reference means a faster card earns more by finishing more jobs, which is the only version of this that cannot be gamed. It also means we never publish a GPU league table, because none is needed.

Two things we are not doing, and why. We do not pay per watt — that pays the least efficient hardware the most, which is a strange thing to reward. And we do not pay for merely being online: your machine is paid for completed work, so a night with no queued jobs pays nothing at all. Both of those are honest limits rather than fine print, which is why they are on this page and not in a footnote.

Your machine stays yours

Pause whenever

Right-click the tray icon and pause for 5 minutes, 10 minutes, an hour, 5 hours, a day, or until you resume. The coordinator is told rather than left guessing, so a job you were holding goes back to the queue immediately instead of stalling until its lease expires.

It stops for your games

The whole point is that it uses the card when you are not. If it is in the way, pause it — and tell us, because a card that has to be paused manually every evening is a bug in our scheduling, not in your habits.

One job at a time

Your GPU is never given two jobs at once, even if you run more than one worker on it. Two jobs sharing 12 GB do not run twice as fast; measured, they turned a 90-second job into 29 minutes. So the rule is one job per card, enforced by the coordinator rather than trusted to the worker.

Uninstalling is a folder delete

Everything — the app, the models, the cache — lives in the one folder you picked. Delete it and the disk is genuinely free again. Your account and balance survive, so reinstalling later picks up where you left off.

What your card is actually asked to do

Jobs come from applications through a public API. Today that means AI text generation and text embeddings; 3D model generation and image generation are built and waiting on workers. You are only ever offered work your machine advertised it can do.

On privacy, in both directions. Jobs carry a sensitivity flag and default to private. A private job's content is not shown to anyone but the machine executing it, and every finished job and its output is deleted after 24 hours — the coordinator is a queue, not an archive. In the other direction: your machine runs somebody else's prompt, and while it is checked for shape and size, we cannot promise you will never be handed something you would rather not have run. If that matters to you, that is a reason not to install this.

Common questions

Is this mining? Does it compete with the PCoin miner?

No, and no. Mining races every other machine for one block reward, so most of the work earns nothing. This is paid work that somebody requested, so every completed job earns. They are also different hardware: PCoin mining is CPU-only by design, and this is GPU-only. You can run both on one machine.

Why does it need a whole 60 GB?

The models are the size, not the app. Several language models at 8–14 billion parameters are tens of gigabytes together, and the more of them your machine holds, the more job types you are eligible for. The app asks before it grows and never writes outside the folder you chose.

My GPU has 8 GB. Can I still earn?

Not yet. The floor is 12 GB because the models we run do not fit below it, and being handed work your card cannot finish would waste your electricity and our queue. If smaller models become worth running, the floor comes down and this page changes.

Do I need to run a PCoin node, or hold PCN first?

Neither. You need an address to be paid to, which the wallet app gives you in about a minute, and that is the only PCoin software involved.

When do I actually get the coins?

Earnings accrue per completed job and are paid out in batches. Your balance, and the split between what is earned and what has been paid, is on the app's account panel. There is a minimum batch size, so a very small balance waits for the next one rather than being sent as dust.

Can I run it on several machines?

Yes. Sign in with the same account on each and they all pay into one balance. Each machine gets its own token, so losing one machine does not affect the others.

I want to send work to the network, not earn from it.

That is the other half of the same system. The API is documented at pcnearner.pc.am/docs — batch submit, poll, collect, priced from the same anchor on this page. Client keys are issued per application; ask and we will issue one.
